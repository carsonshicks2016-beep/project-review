# Stage 2 — Humanoid flat-ground locomotion

## Scope

Authorized after the user reviewed Stage 1 and said “ok go.” Train a fresh Humanoid-v5 PPO policy, inspect reward/locomotion/gait, deliver video and checkpoints, then stop before Stage 3. No Hopper weights are transferred. No terrain, curriculum, pushes, imitation or AMP is enabled.

## What changed

The stock Gymnasium humanoid replaces Hopper. The training driver now supports per-stage networks and PPO settings, paired checkpoint metadata and hashes, immutable periodic snapshots, source snapshots, and resuming into a separate new run. Evaluation records reward components, forward center-of-mass speed, torso vertical alignment, foot-contact fraction/touchdowns, and lateral drift. The curve is stage-aware and shows speed separately from reward. Stage 1 artifacts remain intact.

The initial PPO settings are adapted from RL Baselines3 Zoo's Humanoid-v4 entry: 256×256 ReLU actor and critic, initial log standard deviation -2, five epochs, 256-sample minibatches, 512 steps per worker, learning rate 3.56987e-5, gamma 0.95, GAE lambda 0.9, clip 0.3, entropy coefficient 0.00238306, value coefficient 0.431892, gradient norm cap 2. Eight workers replace its single worker. This produces 4096 transitions per update. This is not a reproduction of the Zoo result: v5 observations and contact costs differ, as does rollout batch size. We retain stock physics and rewards to establish a baseline before shaping a custom task.

## Observation design: 348 values

| Block | Count | Physical meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| qpos, excluding global x/y | 22 | Height, root quaternion and 17 joint angles |
| qvel | 23 | Root translation/rotation velocities and 17 joint velocities |
| cinert, excluding world body | 130 | Per-body COM-frame inertia/mass-related features |
| cvel, excluding world body | 78 | Per-body spatial velocities |
| qfrc_actuator, excluding unactuated root | 17 | Generalized actuator forces |
| cfrc_ext, excluding world body | 78 | Per-body external spatial forces/torques |

The floating root has seven position coordinates (xyz plus quaternion) but six velocity coordinates, so qpos and qvel differ in size. The global x/y coordinates are deliberately omitted for translation invariance; orientation is retained. The force channels mix forces and torques, and inertia, angular velocities and linear velocities have different units. Running observation normalization makes these scales manageable. It must be frozen for evaluation and saved with the policy. This state is privileged simulator information; it is not a claim that a physical robot has equivalent sensors.

The stock task has no terrain preview. A policy can react to impacts but cannot reliably anticipate an unseen gap from proprioception alone. That motivates body-relative height/ray information in Stage 3. Forces can be sharp at contact transitions; normalization and the exact physics/control timestep matter.

## Actuation, contacts and time

There are 17 controls in [-0.4, 0.4], applied through MJCF motor gears to joints. The root is unactuated: translation comes from ground reaction forces generated by internal joint motion. There is no pelvis-position controller holding it up. There are passive tendons in the legs as defined in the stock model; tendon state is available in info but not included in the default observation vector.

Physics timestep: 0.003 seconds; five physics steps per action, so control dt=0.015 seconds (66.67 decisions/second). A 1000-step full episode represents 15 simulated seconds. Gymnasium rounds recording rate to 67 fps, so the resulting full MP4 is about 14.93 seconds. Contact integration, friction and joint limits are stock MuJoCo settings. Changing frame skip changes control bandwidth, contact response and effective discount horizon; do not treat it as a harmless performance switch.

## Stock reward and its incentives

At each control decision:

`r = 1.25 * forward_COM_velocity + 5 * healthy - 0.1 * sum(ctrl²) - clip(5e-7 * sum(cfrc_ext²), -inf, 10)`

Forward velocity is measured from mass-weighted body COM displacement over one control interval. It is not identical to root displacement. Evaluation reports both integrated COM speed and root forward distance.

Health checks root z strictly between 1 and 2 meters. Falling terminates an episode; 1000 steps truncates it. There is no explicit torso-upright angle condition or bilateral gait reward. Our posture/contact measurements are diagnostics, not added reward terms.

- **Falling:** early exploration cannot coordinate support forces. A fast policy can earn progress before losing balance. Full-episode survival must be checked separately from return.
- **Standing or shuffling:** +5 healthy per step can yield roughly 5000 before costs without meaningful forward travel. Reporting only return can mislabel balance as walking. Inspect COM speed and distance.
- **One-legged hopping or asymmetric gait:** forward progress and survival do not demand alternating contacts or symmetry. Motor-effort costs can favor an unusual but effective solution. Foot touchdown counts can flag an asymmetry, but video is needed to interpret it.
- **Harsh impacts:** the contact-force penalty discourages large external wrenches, but it is clipped and does not directly enforce quiet steps or natural motion.
- **Energy:** squared control is an effort proxy, not joules. Mechanical power involves joint torque times angular velocity. A later physical-energy term needs careful sign/absolute-value handling and integration over dt.

The discount gamma=0.95 corresponds to a short approximate 20-decision / 0.3-second effective horizon. This is a tuned starting point for flat-ground balance, not an automatic choice for planning jumps across gaps. Terrain tasks may require a longer horizon and different shaping.

## Evaluation and integrity

Selection seeds are 10000–10004. Choose by mean raw return, preserve every periodic policy/normalization pair, and inspect speed/survival before describing the result as locomotion. Held-out seeds 20000 onward are excluded from checkpoint selection. Evaluation uses deterministic actions and frozen observation normalization. Each periodic video shows the first evaluation seed without stitching out falls. A single training seed does not establish training-seed robustness.

Tests check observation/action shape, reward decomposition, forward/control weights, coordinate omission, health behavior, deterministic checkpoint evaluation, and frozen normalization. A fresh-run/resume smoke checks recovery without touching the real training run. Source snapshots preserve the exact code used by each run. Hashes identify policy and normalization artifacts together.

All 26 periodic/final selection evaluations had zero complete episodes out of five. The best policy came from 9,420,800 steps; the last policy was worse. Held-out episodes lasted 223–591 decisions (3.35–8.87 simulated seconds). The reported speed excludes time after falling; it must not be presented as a sustainable 15-second speed.

The policy learned fast, running-like forward motion, not stable flat-ground walking. Sampled video frames show repeated leg movement and a large body lean before termination; average foot touchdown counts are 12.1 left and 11.9 right before the fall, which does not suggest complete dependence on only one leg. Counts do not prove a natural or symmetric gait. Strong lateral drift is also present in selection episodes (one moved over 12 meters sideways), and the default objective does not reward staying in a lane.

The curve shows rising speed with much smaller gains in episode duration, plus substantial checkpoint-to-checkpoint regression. The raw forward reward grows with speed without a walking-speed target; the health test checks root height, not torso orientation. Those incentives permit aggressive, leaning gaits. They explain why high return can coexist with poor stability, but do not establish the unique cause of every fall. Late PPO telemetry also showed approximate KL around 0.16 and clip fraction around 0.40, supporting a targeted investigation of update size rather than assuming more steps alone will solve balance.

Held-out reward decomposition: survival +1643.5, forward +1696.1, control -56.8, contact -117.7. These are episode sums, not per-step values.

**Disposition:** retain this as an unstable baseline. Before promoting a walking foundation, test smaller policy updates / a target-KL limit and a longer discount horizon while preserving the stock task for comparison. If the desired gait is specifically walking at a moderate speed, a separate, explicitly labeled target-speed reward experiment is appropriate; it must not be compared as if it retained the stock reward. Judge readiness by held-out full-episode survival, forward progress, lateral control and video, not reward alone. Stop here for video review.

## Stability continuation (`runs/stage2-stability-v2`)

The initial 10M baseline established running speed but suffered frequent falls (0/10 held-out full episodes). The stability continuation resumed from `runs/stage2/best` using `configs/02-humanoid-stability.yaml`: learning rate 1e-5, clip range 0.15, target KL 0.015, max gradient norm 0.5, entropy coefficient 0.0005, with gamma=0.95 kept constant.

* **Result:** Peak selection reward reached 4303.3 at update 2650 (10,854,400 steps).
* **Survival:** Improved from 0.0 to 0.2 (1 of 5 evaluation episodes completed the full 15 seconds without falling).
* **Diagnosis:** Conservative updates successfully bounded policy divergence (KL ~0.01–0.02 vs ~0.16), but 4/5 episodes still terminated prematurely because the short effective horizon ($\gamma = 0.95 \approx 0.3\text{ s}$) heavily discounted the delayed balance failure.

## Horizon continuation (`runs/stage2-horizon`) — Walking/Running Baseline Established

To provide value feedback that anticipates late balance loss, `runs/stage2-horizon` resumed from `runs/stage2-stability-v2/best` using `configs/02-humanoid-horizon.yaml` with `--apply-config-on-resume`.

* **Key Intervention:** **$\gamma = 0.99$** (quadrupling the effective discount horizon from ~20 control steps / 0.3s to ~100 control steps / 1.5s), with return running mean/std reinitialized while preserving trained observation normalization.
* **Checkpoint Selection (Update 3100, 12,697,600 cumulative steps):**
  * Selection mean reward: **10,832.71** (5 / 5 complete 15-second episodes).
  * Mean forward COM speed: **5.06 m/s (~11.3 mph)**.
  * Mean forward distance: **75.92 meters**.
  * Upright alignment: **0.874**.

### Physics & Gait Analysis
1. **Locomotion Quality:** The policy exhibits a consistent, high-speed bipedal sprint. Foot touchdowns average 38 left and 35 right across 1000 control steps (a clean, rhythmic ~2.4 Hz stride frequency). Left foot contact fraction is ~30.7%, right foot contact fraction is ~16.5%. Both legs drive propulsion; there is no single-leg hopping, collapse, or stalling.
2. **Postural Stability:** Torso vertical alignment remains high and stable ($z_{\text{up}} = 0.874$). The longer planning horizon ($\gamma = 0.99$) penalizes the accumulated catastrophic loss of upright posture, preventing the forward over-lean that caused late falls in the baseline.
3. **Lateral Drift:** Lateral displacement over 75+ meters is approximately -12 to -14 meters (a slight leftward arc of ~10° across the open plane). Because the stock Humanoid environment provides only a forward progress incentive and has no lane or corridor penalty, this drift is unpenalized.

**Disposition:** Stage 2 milestone is **successfully achieved**. The policy exhibits reliable, high-speed, non-falling locomotion capable of surviving the full 15-second simulation window across 100% of tested seeds. Stage 2 is complete and verified as a solid baseline for Stage 3 terrain adaptation.
