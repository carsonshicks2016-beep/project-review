Scenario Presets

k

The Multiplication Factor (k_eff)

Represents the ratio of neutrons in one generation to the previous.

If k = 1 (Critical), the population is steady. If k < 1 (Subcritical), fissions die out. If k > 1 (Supercritical), power grows exponentially.

The Magic of Delayed Neutrons

About 0.65% of neutrons aren't released immediately during fission, but seconds or minutes later by radioactive decay of fission products (Precursors).

Without this tiny delay, nuclear reactors would change power in microseconds—too fast for humans or computers to control!

Reactor vs. Bomb

Commercial reactors use low-enriched uranium (~3-5% U-235). The physics of the U-238 matrix heavily absorbs neutrons before runaway prompt criticality can occur.

A nuclear explosion is physically impossible in a commercial reactor. Bombs require highly enriched material (90%+ U-235) and precise implosion mechanics.
