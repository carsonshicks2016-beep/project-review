Planck's Quantum Revolution

Classical physics predicted that blackbodies would emit infinite energy at short wavelengths (the "Ultraviolet Catastrophe"). Max Planck resolved this in 1900 by proposing that electromagnetic energy is emitted in discrete "quanta" (packets) with energy E = hf. This sparked the quantum revolution.

Wien's Displacement Law

The wavelength of peak emission is inversely proportional to temperature: λpeak = b/T. This is why cool objects emit in the infrared, hot stars (10,000K) look blue (short peak wavelength), and our Sun (5,778K) peaks in the green-yellow visible range.

Stefan-Boltzmann Law

The total radiated power per unit surface area scales with the fourth power of temperature: j* = σT⁴. Doubling an object's temperature increases its emitted power by 16 times! This fundamental law governs the energy balance of planets and stars.
