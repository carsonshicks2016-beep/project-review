The Resonance Myth

Textbooks often claim the bridge collapsed due to simple forced resonance (like pushing a swing). However, the wind was a steady 42 mph, not oscillating. The true cause was aeroelastic flutter, a self-exciting instability where the twisting bridge extracts energy from the steady wind.

Negative Damping

Normally, damping dissipates energy and stops vibrations. But as wind speed increases, the aerodynamic forces created by the bridge's twisting start acting in the same direction as its velocity. The effective damping becomes negative, causing oscillations to grow exponentially.

Modern Solutions

Modern suspension bridges are designed to prevent flutter. They use deep, stiff trusses instead of solid plate girders, aerodynamic slots in the deck to equalize pressure, and tuned mass dampers to artificially increase structural damping (ζ_s).
