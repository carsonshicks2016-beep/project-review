The Physics of Lift & Stall

Bernoulli's Principle

Air flowing over the curved top of a wing accelerates, which according to Bernoulli's equation (p + ½ρv² = const), causes a decrease in pressure. The slower-moving air underneath maintains higher pressure. This pressure difference creates the upward force known as Lift.

Aerodynamic Stall

As Angle of Attack (α) increases, lift increases linearly. However, at the critical angle (usually ~15°), the air can no longer follow the sharp contour of the upper wing. The boundary layer separates, creating a turbulent wake. Lift abruptly plummets and drag skyrockets.

Stall Recovery

When a stall occurs, increasing engine power alone is not enough. The pilot must decrease the Angle of Attack by pushing the nose down. This allows the smooth airflow to reattach to the upper wing surface, restoring lift and ending the stall condition.
