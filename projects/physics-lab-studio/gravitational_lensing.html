Einstein's Prediction

In 1915, Albert Einstein's General Relativity predicted that massive objects warp spacetime, causing light rays to bend. The deflection angle α = 4GM / (c² b). This was famously verified by Arthur Eddington during the 1919 solar eclipse, observing starlight bending around the Sun.

Symmetry & Einstein Rings

When the background source is perfectly aligned directly behind the lensing mass (alignment parameter u = 0), the symmetry of the warped spacetime bends the light equally in all directions, smearing the source into a continuous circle known as an Einstein Ring with infinite theoretical magnification.

Cosmic Magnifying Glass

Astronomers use massive galaxy clusters as natural telescopes. The gravitational magnification allows us to see ultra-distant, incredibly faint galaxies from the early universe that would otherwise be invisible. Lensing also helps map invisible Dark Matter by analyzing the distortions.
