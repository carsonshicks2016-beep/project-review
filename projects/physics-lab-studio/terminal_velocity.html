1. Terminal Velocity

As you fall, gravity accelerates you downward. But air drag pushes up, growing with the square of your speed ($v^2$). When Drag = Weight, acceleration hits zero. You continue falling at a constant terminal velocity.

2. Altitude & Air Density

Air gets thinner at high altitudes ($\rho = \rho_0 e^{-z/8500}$). With less air density, there's less drag. In a Stratos jump from 39km, the air is so thin you can break the sound barrier before reaching terminal velocity!

3. Parachute Deployment Shock

Deploying a parachute suddenly increases your aerodynamic area by 50x. Drag vastly exceeds your weight, causing a massive upward net force. This violent deceleration (up to 5 Gs) slows you to a safe landing speed.
