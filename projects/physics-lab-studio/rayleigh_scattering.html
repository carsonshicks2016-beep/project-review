Physics Explained

1. The 1/λ⁴ Power Law

Lord Rayleigh discovered that scattering intensity is inversely proportional to the fourth power of the wavelength (I ∝ 1/λ⁴). Because blue light has a shorter wavelength (~400 nm) than red light (~700 nm), it scatters roughly (700/400)⁴ ≈ 9.4 times more efficiently. This floods the sky with scattered blue photons while allowing red ones to pass through.

2. Why Sunsets Are Red

At sunset, sunlight passes tangentially through the atmosphere, increasing the optical path length (airmass) up to 40x. Almost all blue and green photons are scattered away out of the beam. The unscattered direct light reaching your eyes is heavily depleted of short wavelengths, leaving brilliant, direct red and orange rays.

3. The Martian Paradox

Earth's atmosphere is dominated by tiny gas molecules (Rayleigh scattering). Mars has a thin CO₂ atmosphere laden with larger, iron-rich dust particles. These cause Mie scattering and absorb blue light, making the Martian daytime sky a butterscotch-red. Paradoxically, at sunset, the forward scattering of these particles creates a beautiful blue halo around the sun!
