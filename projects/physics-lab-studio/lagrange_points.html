⚖️ The Five Lagrange Points

In a two-body rotating system, gravity and centrifugal forces perfectly balance at 5 specific locations. L1, L2, and L3 are saddle points (unstable like balancing a marble on a saddle). L4 and L5 are potential peaks, but curiously stabilized by the Coriolis force if the mass ratio is small enough (μ < 0.0385), forming stable traps for Trojan asteroids.

🔭 Why L2 for JWST?

The James Webb Space Telescope orbits L2 (behind Earth relative to the Sun). Because it's further out, it should orbit slower than Earth, but Earth's extra gravitational pull speeds it up so it stays locked with Earth. L2 allows JWST to keep the Sun, Earth, and Moon always behind its heat shield, while it maintains an unstable "halo orbit" requiring occasional thruster station-keeping.

🎢 Jacobi Energy & Topography

The Jacobi Constant ($C_J$) is the conserved "energy" in this rotating frame. It defines forbidden regions (Zero-Velocity Curves). If a satellite's $C_J$ is high, it's trapped near a planet. As $C_J$ lowers, "necks" open at L1 and L2, allowing interplanetary travel (the Interplanetary Transport Network).
