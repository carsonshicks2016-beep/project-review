1 Optical Path & Phase Flip

Light reflects off the top and bottom of the thin film. The bottom reflection travels an extra distance (Optical Path Difference = 2nd·cos(θ)). Because the top surface reflection happens going from a low index (air) to higher index (film), it undergoes a 180° (π) phase shift.

2 The "Black Film"

As gravity drains the liquid, the top of the bubble gets extremely thin (< 30nm). Because the extra path length becomes zero, only the 180° phase flip remains. All wavelengths destructively interfere! The film appears black right before it pops.

3 Anti-Reflective Coatings

Glasses use thin films (often n=1.38, d=138nm) where BOTH reflections undergo a 180° phase shift (air -> coating -> glass). They tune thickness to λ/4 for green light (~550nm). The reflections are 180° out of phase, canceling glare perfectly!
