1. The Evanescent Wave

Classically, a particle bounces off a barrier if it lacks the energy to go over. Quantum mechanics treats particles as waves. When a probability wave hits a barrier, it doesn't stop instantly; it decays exponentially inside the forbidden region as an evanescent wave.

2. Exponential Sensitivity

The transmission probability $T \propto e^{-2\kappa L}$. Because it is exponential, tiny changes in barrier width ($L$) or height cause massive changes in tunneling rate. This explains why radioactive half-lives vary from nanoseconds to billions of years!

3. Real-World Tech (STM)

Scanning Tunneling Microscopes use this effect. By bringing a sharp tip close to a surface, electrons tunnel across the vacuum gap. The extreme sensitivity of the tunneling current to distance allows mapping surfaces atom-by-atom.
