1. Interference Sharpening

With 2 slits (Young's experiment), interference produces broad, fuzzy fringes. As the number of illuminated slits (N) increases, the primary maxima sharpen dramatically while secondary maxima disappear. Peak width is proportional to 1/N.

2. Stellar Fingerprints

Chemical elements emit or absorb light at specific discrete wavelengths determined by their atomic energy levels. Passing starlight through a grating reveals these specific spectral lines, acting as a chemical barcode across light-years.

3. Dispersion & Resolving Power

Dispersion (spreading out colors) depends on grating density (lines/mm). Resolving power R = λ/Δλ = m*N measures the ability to distinguish closely spaced spectral lines. High N is critical for precision spectroscopy.
