1. The Gyroscopic Myth

For a long time, people believed bicycles stayed upright solely because the spinning wheels acted like gyroscopes. While gyroscopic precession does help steer the front wheel into a lean, it's not strictly necessary.

As Kooijman et al. demonstrated in 2011, a bicycle with counter-rotating wheels (canceling all gyroscopic effects) can still perfectly balance itself! Try the "Zero Gyro" preset to see it in action.

2. Caster Trail & Steering into Leans

The real hero of bicycle stability is the Steering Geometry (Trail). The front wheel touches the ground behind the steering axis.

When the bike leans to the right, ground reaction forces push the contact patch, twisting the handlebars to the right. The bike automatically steers into the fall. Forward motion then generates centrifugal force, pushing the bike back upright!

3. Counter-Steering

Because a bike must lean to turn at high speeds, humans use a counter-intuitive method to initiate turns.

To turn right, you briefly steer left. The centrifugal force tips you to the right, inducing a lean. Once leaned over, you steer right to match the lean and maintain the turn. The self-stability physics handles the rest!
