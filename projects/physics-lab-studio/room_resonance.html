What Are Standing Waves?

When a sound wave hits a wall, it reflects. If the distance between walls matches a multiple of half the wavelength, the reflected wave perfectly overlaps the original. This creates a "standing wave" that doesn't seem to move. The room itself becomes a resonant instrument, boosting some frequencies (antinodes) and killing others (nodes).

The Low Frequency Problem

Bass wavelengths are huge (e.g., 50Hz = ~6.8 meters long). Small rooms only support a few resonant modes at these low frequencies, leaving huge gaps between resonant peaks. This causes a very "lumpy" frequency response where some bass notes boom uncontrollably while others completely vanish depending on where you sit.

Why Corners Are Loudest

Because walls are rigid boundaries, air particles can't move through them. This means velocity is zero at the walls, which physically forces the pressure to be maximum! Since corners are the intersection of three walls, they contain the maximum pressure for *every single room mode*. This is why bass traps are always placed in corners.
