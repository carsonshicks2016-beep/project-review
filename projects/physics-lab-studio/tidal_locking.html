1. Two Tidal Bulges

Gravity weakens with distance. The planet pulls the moon's near side harder than its center, and the center harder than its far side. This differential pull stretches the moon into an ellipsoid, creating two bulges facing toward and away from the planet.

2. Tidal Torque & Lag

If the moon spins faster than its orbit, friction pulls the tidal bulges slightly ahead of the planet-moon line (lag angle δ). The planet's gravity pulls back on this misaligned bulge, creating a torque that slows the moon's spin down over millions of years.

3. Eccentricity & Resonance

In an elliptical orbit, orbital speed varies (Kepler's 2nd Law). Tidal forces are strongest at periapsis (closest approach). If eccentricity is high enough, the moon can lock its spin to match the orbital speed *only at periapsis*, leading to higher-order resonances like Mercury's 3:2.
