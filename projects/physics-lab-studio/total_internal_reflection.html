Snell's Law & TIR

When light enters a lower-index medium (n₁ > n₂), it bends away from the normal. Because of this, the refracted angle reaches 90° before the incident angle does. The incident angle where this happens is the Critical Angle. Beyond this, 100% of the light reflects internally.

n₁·sin(θ₁) = n₂·sin(θ₂)
θ_crit = arcsin(n₂ / n₁)

Fiber Optics

Optical fibers consist of a pure glass core surrounded by a cladding with a slightly lower refractive index. By injecting light at a shallow angle, it undergoes repeated Total Internal Reflection. This traps the light, allowing data pulses to travel thousands of kilometers under oceans with virtually no loss, carrying 99% of internet traffic.

Evanescent Waves & Diamonds

During TIR, light doesn't just bounce off the boundary hard—an evanescent wave slightly penetrates the second medium and decays exponentially.

Also, why do diamonds sparkle? With a high index (n=2.42), the critical angle is tiny (~24°). Light entering a diamond gets trapped, bouncing around multiple internal facets before exiting, creating brilliant sparkles!
