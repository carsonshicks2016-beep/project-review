Space itself is stretching

Cosmological redshift is often confused with the Doppler effect, but they are physically distinct. Galaxies aren't rushing away through pre-existing space; rather, the spacetime metric between them is expanding. As a photon travels, the space it occupies stretches, literally pulling its wavelength apart over billions of years. 1+z = λ_obs / λ_emit = a(t_now) / a(t_then)

Faster than light?

Hubble's Law states v = H₀ × d. Beyond the Hubble Sphere (~14 billion light-years), galaxies recede from us faster than light (v > c). This doesn't violate Special Relativity because SR only forbids objects moving past each other locally faster than c. The expansion rate is an accumulation of space being created everywhere in between.

The flash of the Big Bang

The Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) at z ≈ 1100 is the oldest light we can see. Emitted 380,000 years after the Big Bang when the universe cooled enough to form atoms, it started as brilliant 3000K orange visible light. Space has stretched by a factor of 1100 since then, cooling the light into invisible 2.7K microwaves we detect today as cosmic static.
