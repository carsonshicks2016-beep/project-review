Why Lightning "Steps"

Air is an insulator. Lightning doesn't flow continuously; it builds up localized electric fields until they exceed the dielectric breakdown threshold (~3 MV/m). A plasma channel forms, charge surges forward about 50 meters, pauses for ~50 microseconds, then builds up again.

The Upward Return Stroke

As the downward stepped leader approaches ground, the intense electric field pulls positive "streamers" up from tall objects. When they connect, the circuit closes. The massive return stroke travels UPWARD at 1/3 the speed of light, heating the channel to 30,000 K!

Franklin's Lightning Rod

Lightning rods don't just "catch" lightning; they enhance the local electric field to launch positive upward streamers early. By providing a predictable connection point and a safe path to ground, they prevent the strike from passing through the building itself.
