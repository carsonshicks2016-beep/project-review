What are Chladni Figures?

Discovered by Ernst Chladni in 1787, these patterns reveal the complex vibrational modes of rigid surfaces. Napoleon Bonaparte was so fascinated by them that he offered a prize to the first person who could mathematically explain the phenomena, which was won by Sophie Germain in 1816.

The Physics of Sand

When the plate vibrates, some regions move rapidly up and down (antinodes) while other lines remain perfectly still (nodes). Sand grains scattered on the plate are repeatedly bounced off the antinodes and naturally accumulate along the quiet nodal lines, revealing the geometry of the sound.

Modern Applications

While visually beautiful, this principle is crucial in engineering. Luthiers use Chladni patterns to tune the top and back plates of violins and guitars. Engineers use similar modal analysis to ensure airplane wings and bridge components don't tear themselves apart at resonant frequencies.
