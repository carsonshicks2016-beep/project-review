1. Differential Gravity

Spaghettification happens because gravity follows an inverse-square law. The pull on an object's feet is stronger than on its head. This difference (tidal force) stretches it radially and compresses it laterally.

2. The Mass Paradox

Counter-intuitively, supermassive black holes are safer to approach! Tidal forces at the horizon scale with $1/M^2$. You can cross a supermassive black hole's horizon unharmed, while a small stellar black hole will shred you millions of miles away.

3. Relativity of Time

To an outside observer, the falling astronaut appears to slow down and freeze at the horizon, their light redshifting to infinity. To the astronaut (proper time), they cross the horizon in a finite amount of time, noticing nothing unusual until they approach the singularity.
