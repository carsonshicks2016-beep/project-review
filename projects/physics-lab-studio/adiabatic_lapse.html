1. Expansion & Cooling

As an air parcel rises, atmospheric pressure drops. By the First Law of Thermodynamics, the parcel expands. Doing work to expand against the surrounding air consumes internal energy, causing its temperature to drop. For dry air, this is the Dry Adiabatic Lapse Rate (~9.8 °C/km).

2. Condensation & Latent Heat

Cooler air holds less water vapor. At the Lifting Condensation Level (LCL), relative humidity hits 100%. Vapor condenses into liquid (clouds), releasing latent heat. This warms the parcel, reducing the cooling rate to the Saturated Adiabatic Lapse Rate (~4-6 °C/km).

3. Topography & Rain Shadow

When mountains force air upwards (orographic lift), it often rains out its moisture. As the dried air descends the leeward slope, it warms at the faster dry rate (9.8 °C/km). It arrives at the bottom significantly warmer and drier than it started—a Chinook or Foehn wind.
