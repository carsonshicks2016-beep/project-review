1. Maxwell's Paradox (1867)

James Clerk Maxwell imagined a tiny "demon" guarding a trapdoor between gas chambers. By only letting fast molecules go right and slow ones left, the demon seemingly creates a temperature difference without doing mechanical work—challenging the Second Law of Thermodynamics.

2. Szilard Engine (1929)

Leo Szilard simplified the paradox, showing that the demon must measure the particle's state. He linked thermodynamics to information theory, suggesting the act of measurement or information processing itself is part of the physical system's entropy balance.

3. Landauer's Principle (1961)

Rolf Landauer proved that "Information is Physical." The measurement doesn't necessarily cost energy, but erasing the demon's memory to repeat the cycle does! Erasing 1 bit dissipates at least k_B T ln(2) of heat, perfectly balancing the entropy equation.
