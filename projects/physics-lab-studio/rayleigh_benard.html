The Rayleigh Number

The Rayleigh number (Ra) measures the ratio of buoyancy forces (which drive fluid motion) to viscous and thermal dissipation forces (which resist motion). When Ra is below a critical threshold (~1708), viscosity wins and heat transfers via pure conduction. Above it, buoyancy takes over, creating convection cells.

2

Bénard Cells

As the instability triggers, the fluid self-organizes into counter-rotating rolls to maximize heat transfer efficiency. In a 3D fluid, these often form beautiful hexagonal honeycomb patterns (Bénard cells), where warm fluid rises in the center and sinks at the edges.

3

Universal Physics

This exact same physics applies across vastly different scales. From heating a pot of miso soup on the stove, to atmospheric thermals that birds ride, to the churning mantle of the Earth driving plate tectonics, and even the granular surface of the Sun (solar granulation).
