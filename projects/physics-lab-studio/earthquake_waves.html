Why S-Waves Stop

S-waves are shear (transverse) waves. Their speed depends on the shear modulus ($\mu$). Liquids have a shear modulus of zero because they cannot sustain shear stress—they simply flow. Thus, $v_S = \sqrt{\mu/\rho} = 0$, completely blocking S-waves at the core-mantle boundary!

The Shadow Zones

The liquid outer core stops S-waves entirely, casting a massive "shadow" from 104° to 180° globally. P-waves enter the liquid core but refract (bend) sharply due to the density/speed change, creating a ring-shaped P-wave shadow zone from 104° to 140°.

Epicenter Triangulation

Because P-waves travel faster than S-waves, the gap between their arrival times ($t_S - t_P$) increases with distance. By reading this lag from a seismogram, we get the distance. Intersecting distance circles from 3 stations reveals the exact epicenter!
