PURE WAVE (SUPERPOSITION)
Particles exhibit wave-like interference. Which-way information is entirely unknown.

1. Single-Particle Interference

Even when fired one by one, each particle passes through both slits simultaneously as a probability wave ψ(x). It interferes with itself! The interference pattern only emerges statistically over time as thousands of individual particles land on the screen according to the probability distribution P(x) = |ψ₁ + ψ₂|².

2. The Measurement Problem

If we place a detector at the slits to see which way the particle goes, the quantum superposition is destroyed. The wavefunction "collapses" into a definite state. The probability becomes classical: P(x) = P₁ + P₂. The interference fringes instantly vanish, replaced by two classical particle bands.

3. Decoherence & The Born Rule

The Born Rule gives the probability density. When a measurement is partially certain (Decoherence parameter D), the equation is:
P(x) = P₁ + P₂ + 2(1-D)√(P₁P₂)cos(kΔr)
D=0 means pure wave (fringes). D=1 means pure particle (no fringes). This smooth transition is quantum decoherence.
