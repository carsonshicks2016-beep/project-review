Standing Waves

When two identical sound waves travel in opposite directions, they interfere to form a standing wave. The pattern doesn't appear to move. It has nodes (points of zero pressure change) and antinodes (points of maximum pressure change).

2

Acoustic Radiation

Sound waves exert a small physical force called acoustic radiation pressure. In a standing wave, the Gor'kov potential describes how particles are pushed away from pressure antinodes and trapped at the nodes, forming a potential well.

3

Applications

Acoustic levitation enables containerless processing. It's used in pharmaceuticals to create pure amorphous drugs, in studying fluid dynamics without solid boundaries, and in handling hazardous or highly reactive materials.
