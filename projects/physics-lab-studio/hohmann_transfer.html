1. The Hohmann Transfer

The most fuel-efficient way to move between two circular orbits. It uses an elliptical orbit that exactly grazes both the starting orbit (at periapsis) and the target orbit (at apoapsis). It requires two precise engine burns (Δv).

2. Launch Windows & Synodic Period

Because planets move at different speeds, you can't launch anytime. You must wait for the correct "phase angle" so the target planet arrives at the intercept point at the same time you do. For Earth-Mars, this window opens every 26 months (synodic period).

3. Real-World Limitations

Hohmann transfers are slow. For outer planets (Jupiter, Saturn), transit times take decades. Real missions often use faster trajectories (requiring more fuel) or gravitational slingshots (gravity assists) to steal momentum from other planets to speed up.
