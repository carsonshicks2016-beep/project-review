Lenz's Law

"Nature abhors a change in flux." As the magnet falls, its changing magnetic field induces swirling eddy currents in the conducting tube walls. These currents create their own magnetic field that exactly opposes the magnet's motion, generating a powerful upward braking force.

Kinetic to Heat

Where does the falling kinetic energy go? It is converted directly into heat! The induced currents face electrical resistance in the metal tube (Ohm's Law: P = I²R). The mechanical energy is dissipated as Joule heating in the tube walls.

Applications

This isn't just a party trick. Eddy current brakes are frictionless, wear-free, and their stopping power scales with speed. They are used in the emergency brakes of Shinkansen bullet trains, high-speed roller coasters, and magnetic rowing machines.
