1. Mass is Destiny

A star's life is a constant battle between gravity (pulling in) and fusion pressure (pushing out) — a state called hydrostatic equilibrium. The initial mass dictates core temperature, which determines fusion rate. Massive stars burn their fuel furiously fast ($L \propto M^{3.5}$) and die young, while low-mass red dwarfs can live for trillions of years!

2. The Iron Catastrophe

Massive stars fuse heavier and heavier elements in "onion shells" (H $\rightarrow$ He $\rightarrow$ C $\rightarrow$ O $\rightarrow$ Si) until they reach Iron-56. Iron has the highest nuclear binding energy; fusing it absorbs energy rather than releasing it. The core loses outward pressure and collapses in milliseconds, rebounding as a catastrophic Type II Supernova.

3. Quantum Degeneracy Limits

When fusion stops, what holds gravity back? Quantum physics! Electron degeneracy pressure supports White Dwarfs up to the Chandrasekhar limit (1.44 $M_\odot$). Neutron degeneracy pressure supports Neutron Stars up to the TOV limit (~2.2 $M_\odot$). Beyond this, nothing stops gravity, forming a Black Hole.
