# Validation protocol

This protocol is preregistered before the first production run. Any deviation goes in the run report with the reason and the effect on interpretation.

## Stage A — input and solver verification

1. Build exactly IBAMR `v0.19.0`; retain commit ID, compiler/MPI/dependency versions, configure logs, and build logs. `tools/build_solver_reference.sh` builds the upstream 3-D staggered Navier–Stokes regression app from the IBAMR source tree.
2. Run `navier_stokes_01_3d` with its version-matched input and expected output. Preserve both files, executable/source hashes, stdout/stderr, and six velocity/pressure error norms. A moth run cannot substitute for these checks.
3. Prepare stationary and matched-translation controls with `tools/prepare_verification.py`; the matrix has three AMR resolutions and two timestep ceilings. Matched translation uses an initially uniform 10 m/s flow and translates both surfaces at the same speed for 0.4 ms (4 mm). Its continuum solution has zero relative flow while the target points move across AMR regions. Stationary and zero-amplitude controls are exact-flow sanity checks.
   Before `initializePatchHierarchy()`, install the registered component BCs on every existing `INSStaggeredVelocityBcCoef` wrapper as well as calling `registerPhysicalBoundaryConditions()`. IBAMR 0.19 constructs those wrappers before application registration, and `advanceHierarchy()` performs regridding before the later preprocessing refresh; otherwise early tagging/regrid fills can silently use default homogeneous Dirichlet conditions. Retain a run-time check or analytic control showing that the physical-boundary coefficients used by the first refinement match the input.
4. With `ENABLE_VERIFICATION_DIAGNOSTICS=TRUE`, the app writes timestep, realized fluid CFL, Lagrangian target-position error, immersed-boundary no-slip velocity error, and composite-grid divergence norms to `force_history.csv`. No-slip error compares IBAMR's interpolated Lagrangian `U` after the advance with analytic target velocity at the step midpoint; it reports nodal RMS/max and normalization by characteristic prescribed speed. Divergence comes from the registered staggered `Div_U` field and is volume-weighted over composite AMR cells, excluding covered coarse cells. The surface-band norm uses actual immersed points and cell centers within two finest-grid cell widths, with composite cell weights to exclude covered coarse cells. These diagnostics were exercised in the prior Stage A runs; the new physical-boundary momentum ledger still requires a fresh smoke and analytic-control check.
5. `momentum_budget.csv` records the whole-domain fluid momentum rate, the sum of the two wing forces from the existing IBAMR moving-control-volume evaluator, and separately integrated physical-boundary pressure traction, advective momentum flux, and viscous traction. The outer-boundary quadrature uses the staggered MAC fields and is independently coded rather than reconstructed from the evaluator's force identity. Before traction evaluation, copy the measured velocity to a diagnostic side field and fill physical and coarse-fine ghosts with the registered component BCs at the measurement time; never use stale ghosts from the live current field. It integrates each AMR level's physical-boundary faces and excludes coarse faces covered by the next finer level. Its sign convention is `dP_fluid/dt + F_hydrodynamic_on_body - (T_pressure + T_advection + T_viscous) = 0`, where `T_advection = -rho*u*(u dot n)` and pressure/viscous terms are outward stress traction. The output flags whether fine-level boundary faces were present. The force and independent flux integrals share the same computed flow solution, so closure tests conservation/measurement consistency, not solution accuracy.
6. Before interpreting the moving matrix, apply these fixed Stage A tolerances: target-position RMS ≤ 2% of finest `dx` and max ≤ 10% of `dx`; no-slip velocity RMS ≤ 5% and max ≤ 20% of reference speed; normalized divergence RMS `L/U_ref` ≤ `1e-4` and max ≤ `1e-3`. Each error must also decrease or reach a documented solver/kernel error floor under mesh and timestep refinement. These are numerical-method gates, not biological validation tolerances.
7. Check that all immersed points remain in the active hierarchy for every prepared control. Wake refinement and far-field retention remain Stage B checks. The zero-amplitude control must not produce periodic lift or nonzero work above solver/interpolation error.

The initial matched-translation smokes exceeded the predeclared no-slip RMS and divergence limits. They are failed local diagnostics, not passed moving-boundary controls. The no-immersed-boundary-force uniform-flow control is prepared with `tools/prepare_force_isolation.py`: it keeps geometry, grid, initialization, boundary conditions, and timestep fixed, sets target stiffness to zero, and omits `IBStandardForceGen`, while retaining the force evaluator. The first-step force persisted within 0.017%. Composite summaries show that the uniform state changes during regridding/projection/synchronization, before the fluid timestep: mean `u_x` falls from 10.00 to 9.28 m/s and divergence reaches `0.240 s^-1` RMS / `39.34 s^-1` maximum. After advancing, mean `u_x` remains 9.28 m/s. The evaluator's `4.44 kN` result is mostly canceled by a `−4.44 kN` control-volume momentum-rate term, leaving a `0.0528 N` inferred surface-flux term; an independent level-zero closed-surface integral gives `0.0494 N` in x, but has per-wing transverse discrepancies and is not grid-converged. Next, compare a matched no-force single-level run with the AMR run before modifying solver settings. Stage A remains failed; do not start the longer moth hover pilot until the regrid/projection failure is repaired and the declared error trend is demonstrated.

The target tolerances are expressed as fractions of the finest cell width to constrain sub-grid boundary lag. Velocity and divergence tolerances are nondimensionalized by the prescribed speed and characteristic radius. In this first implementation, the manufactured-solution regression is an independent pass/fail solver check, but its six published field-error norms do not directly calibrate moving-boundary interpolation or divergence tolerances. Treat the listed values as preregistered engineering gates, retain that calibration limitation, and do not retune them after seeing moth results.

No biological interpretation is permitted until Stage A passes.

## Stage B — moth case and numerical repeatability

Use a fixed physical model and boundary conditions. Set `dt_max` so there are at least 256 steps per wingbeat on the coarsest temporal case; halve it for each successive time refinement, and demonstrate that the adaptive/CFL controller actually respects the intended ceiling. For any stability-limited smaller step, record the realized distribution.

Run a matrix of three spatial resolutions by three time-step ceilings. Spatial levels change the finest Eulerian spacing by factors of two (coarse / medium / fine); time-step ceilings likewise halve (coarse / medium / fine). Preserve domain extents, physical parameters, immersed-boundary kernel, point-density ratio, target stiffness, and refinement/tagging criteria. A stiffness/point-spacing sensitivity may be added as a separate axis; it cannot replace the required grid and time comparisons.

Each case continues through startup until the cycle-resolved force and power histories for three consecutive complete wingbeats agree pairwise within 2%. Compare phase histories on a common phase grid using normalized L2 and maximum absolute difference. The normalization floor, phase bins, excluded startup cycles, and sign convention are recorded. If this condition is not reached within resource limits, the periodic-response gate fails and the stop point is reported.

For the two finest spatial resolutions and separately for the two finest time-step cases, cycle-mean vertical force and aerodynamic power must differ by no more than 5%. Also report phase-resolved force and power differences, not only means. Report force-axis mapping, cycle averaging window, and both absolute and normalized differences. The 5% condition is necessary, not sufficient: phase traces must be compared and any large local deviations explained.

Record wall time, CPU time if available, peak resident memory, MPI ranks, maximum concurrent workers, disk usage before and after, and output sizes. Stop a local run before solver RSS exceeds 8 GB or free disk would fall below 40 GB.

## Stage C — published force comparison

Compare the rigid base-kinematics case to Nakata & Liu (2012) using its own body/wing force convention. The paper reports cycle means of 15.4 mN vertical and −4.4 mN horizontal for the rigid-base model, and 20.8 / 9.9 mN vertical and 13.5 / −22.2 mN horizontal over down/up strokes. The current implementation omits body geometry and therefore only gives wing forces. Its history cannot satisfy this literature gate until a body representation is added or a comparable wing-only reference is obtained.

Digitize the published force-history figure from the highest-quality accessible source, preserve the source image, the pixel-to-axis transform, digitized points, operator/script version, and a digitization uncertainty estimate. Compare harmonics, reversal timing, down/up-stroke asymmetry, peaks/plateaus, and cycle means. Distinguish raster digitization uncertainty from numerical error and source-model differences. A mean-force match alone is not validation.

## Stage E — decision

The only acceptable status values are:

- `VALIDATED_WITHIN_DECLARED_SCOPE`: all numerical, repeatability, convergence, literature, and independent-PIV gates passed, with no unexplained key discrepancy.
- `UNVALIDATED`: any gate failed or remains unevaluated; the report identifies the failure and preserves the result.
- `NOT_RUN`: case not executed.

There is no “mostly validated” status. None of these statuses implies a trained controller can hover or fly.

## Target-point stiffness and damping diagnosis (2026-10-02)

IBAMR 0.19.0's `IBTargetPointForceSpec` computes `F = k(X_target-X) - eta U`; the application had specified stiffness but left damping at its default zero. The pinned IBAMR explicit cylinder example chooses `eta=2*sqrt(K*M)` for critical damping, with its effective marker mass based on the regularized-delta support. A separate fixed-target, initially uniform 10 m/s crossflow study tested K=1, 10, and 30 N/m per point without damping, then K=1 with the critical-damping estimate `eta=2.00256e-4 kg/s/point` and its half-dt counterpart. For the 3-D case, `M=rho/(C/dx)^3` with `C=3/8` is a dimensional extension of the upstream 2-D example and is explicitly exploratory.

No stiffness/damping combination passed the predeclared no-slip/divergence gates. Undamped K=1/10/30 produced large no-slip error and transient multi-newton oscillations; the K30 case was stopped at 37.4 us after rapidly increasing force/divergence. Critical damping reduced the K1 coarse-dt aggregate momentum residual to `2.50e-4` of term magnitudes, but no-slip remained above `Uref` transiently and divergence peaked at 3030 s^-1. At half dt, the critical-damping residual rose to `9.01e-3` and divergence peaked at 388 s^-1; this is not a convergent trend. The half-dt undamped K10 case was also stopped at 21 us after a growing multi-newton force. All stop records remain intact with adjacent `interpretation.json` files; quantitative outcomes are in `results/stage_a_postfix_matched_translation_matrix/target_penalty_coupling_study.json`.

At this point in the investigation, the input template and preparer exposed/hashed `TARGET_DAMPING` but rejected it for translating targets because IBAMR's built-in term damped absolute interpolated velocity. This interim restriction was superseded by the later relative-velocity reference shift documented below. Stage A remained **FAILED / UNVALIDATED**; no moth hover or PPO run was performed.

## Relative target damping for prescribed motion (2026-10-02, supersedes the earlier preparer restriction)

IBAMR's built-in target spring uses `k*(X_ref-X)-eta*U_fluid`. Before this correction, the app left `eta=0` because enabling the built-in damping directly would brake a moving wing in the laboratory frame. The app now shifts the reference seen by the force calculation to `X_prescribed+(eta/k)*U_target` during the explicit midpoint force stage, then analytically restores the physical geometric reference before endpoint kinematics and diagnostics. Thus the force is algebraically `k*(X_prescribed-X)-eta*(U_fluid-U_target)`. Translation uses constant prescribed velocity; the existing hover Euler-angle law has an analytic derivative implementation. This retains IBAMR's target-point force strategy and adds no empirical aerodynamic term.

A damped matched-translation null (`K=1 N/m/point`, `eta=2.00256e-4 kg/s/point`) completed 0.4 ms / 4 mm at N64, level 3, coarse dt and crossed multiple regrids. It remained exact to roundoff: target lag `7.82e-13 dx`, no-slip `1.24e-15 Uref`, zero sampled divergence, and maximum force `4.72e-16 N`; maximum domain-ledger residual was `1.69e-7 N` (`5.87e-7` characteristic-scaled). Runtime was 213.3 s, peak RSS 3.90 GiB, and 75.1 GB free disk remained. Artifacts, configuration, source/executable hashes, histories, and resource record are in `results/stage_a_postfix_matched_translation_matrix/relative_damping_matched_translation_long/` and summarized in `relative_target_damping_summary.json`.

This is a prescribed constant-translation kinematics check only; no rotating wing or hawkmoth hover was run. ## Pinned IBAMR force and coupling source audit (2026-10-03)

Inspection of the installed-source-matched IBAMR 0.19.0 implementation clarified the terminology. The app registers its callback with IIM FE velocity data. The callback force enters `IIMethod::computeLagrangianForce()`, which forms pressure and velocity-gradient jumps; with both jump options enabled in the open-disk input, the direct residual force is removed and the jump contributions are inserted through `imposeJumpConditions()`. This is a force/jump prescription, not an unknown traction multiplier solving a no-slip constraint. `use_direct_forcing` in `IBFESurfaceMethod` holds coordinates by copying current positions; it does not set the Eulerian interface velocity.

`TAU_OUT` is specifically IBAMR's exterior-side traction system: `WSS_OUT - P_OUT*n`, with normal orientation inherited from the mesh. The version-matched closed-body `examples/IIM/ex5` integrates it as net body force. The open disk has no enclosed interior and `TAU_OUT` is only one oriented side; its integral is not established as the total force on a two-sided wing. It still needs correct two-sided accounting and independent momentum closure.

The app's postprocessor now separates the spring and velocity-feedback terms, the combined registered IIM callback resultant, and the `TAU_OUT` traction resultant. Length, velocity and area use code-unit labels because the input has no SI calibration. Fresh `ETA_S=0` and `ETA_S=100` 10-step runs reproduce the earlier mismatch values to printed precision; the callback decomposition sums as expected. These are diagnostic pipeline checks, not force validation. Full source audit and hashes: `docs/iim_force_accounting_audit.md`, `results/open_disk_force_audit_local_20261003_v1/study_summary.json`, and `artifact_manifest.json`. Stage A remains **FAILED / UNVALIDATED**.
